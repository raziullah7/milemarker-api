## 4. Differences from the brief

- `places.py` has two additions beyond the skeleton, both public: `use_cities_file(path | None)` (the brief asked for such a function) and `is_hand_added(city, state)`, which the loader needs to count "Placed from hand-added places" without duplicating the table logic. It also exports `COVERED_STATES` (the lower 48 and DC), used by the loader for its "in the lower 48" filter, so the coverage list exists once. No skeleton signature changed.
- The loader points the lookup at `--cities` only for its own run and restores the default in a `finally`. Tests also reset it in fixtures.
- Rows for Alaska, Hawaii and Puerto Rico are left out of the in-memory places table, so suggestions never offer a place the API would refuse.
- Hand-added places apply only when the table lacks the city, so `Henrico, NC` is unaffected (PL-13).
- When one city appears with different spellings that normalise to one key, the label uses the first row's spelling.

## 5. Unclear or untestable in the documents

- Architecture 6 says the country test applies to a "final part". I applied it to the last comma-separated part only, so `Tijuana Mexico` (no comma) is an unknown place, not outside coverage. The documents give no case for this.
- "Closest names in that state" has no stated measure. I used `difflib.get_close_matches` (default cutoff 0.6, best first). With no state, suggestions are the first five states alphabetically by code; the documents do not say how to choose among more than five.
- `nearest_station` returns the distance of the chosen station, which can differ by up to 0.01 mile from the true minimum.

## 6. Not sure

- Suggestion quality for badly misspelt names rests on difflib's cutoff; only PL-16 checks it.
- The state-name-alone rule (`Texas`, `Washington`) treats the text as a city for suggestions, so `Washington` suggests `Washington, DC` and similar.
- The dev database was migrated with `migrate`; the real stations are not loaded into it (tests load and roll back their own).
